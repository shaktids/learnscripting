Bug bounding refers to the process of identifying the scope and impact of a software bug or issue. The goal of bug bounding is to determine the root cause of the bug, understand its behavior, and assess its potential impact on the software system and its users.

Bug bounding typically involves analyzing the symptoms and effects of the bug, as well as any related error messages, logs, or other relevant information. This can involve testing the software in different scenarios and environments, and gathering data and feedback from users and stakeholders.

Bug bounding is an important part of the software development process, as it helps developers prioritize and allocate resources to fix bugs based on their severity and impact. By identifying and bounding bugs early on, developers can minimize the risk of introducing new bugs or issues, and ensure that the software is as stable and reliable as possible.
